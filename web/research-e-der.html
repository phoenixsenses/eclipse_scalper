Exhaustion Rebound

A structural account of what happens after forced selling runs out of participants. It is Eclipse's principal research candidate and it is not a deployed strategy.

Research candidate 3 frozen arms Not deployed

01 The mechanism

Forced sellers are not choosing to sell.

A liquidation cascade is supply from participants who have no discretion: their positions are being closed for them. That supply is large, price-insensitive and finite.

The interesting moment is not the cascade. It is the second one — the echo — arriving while the market is still stressed from the first. If the market absorbs that second wave anyway, the population of forced sellers has plausibly been exhausted rather than paused. The conditions that decide "absorbed" are not published.

What follows is a rebound, and the claim E-DER actually makes is about its timing: the recovery is delayed rather than immediate. That delay is the whole hypothesis, and it is also the part most likely to be an artifact.

02 Design constraints

Every degree of freedom removed on purpose.

The easiest way to make a rebound study look profitable is to let the exit float. E-DER cannot: every timing decision is frozen in advance, and none of it responds to how the trade is going.

That makes the arms worse on paper than a tuned version would look, and it is the only way the result means anything.

Frozen by construction

Entry, exit, holding period and every other timing decision are fixed in advance and recorded before prospective tracking begins. Nothing is chosen per event, nothing adapts, and nothing is revised once the arm is running.

The specific values are not published. What matters publicly is that there are no discretionary knobs left to turn — which is what makes a forward result mean anything, and what makes it impossible to rescue a failing arm by adjusting it.

03 The three arms

Each arm relaxes exactly one thing.

The arms are a controlled experiment on the definition itself. Each one differs from its neighbour by exactly one condition. Because only one thing moves at a time, a difference between arms is attributable — which is the entire reason for running three.

Why cascade count matters more than event count
Events inside one cascade are not independent observations. Ten events from a single cascade are closer to one observation than to ten, so V3's 198 events rest on 10 cascades — and the cascade column is the one that governs how much can be concluded.

04 How it dies

Written down before the answer is known.

A hypothesis that cannot be killed is not a hypothesis. These conditions were recorded in advance, and any one of them closes E-DER regardless of how the arms happen to be performing at the time.

Prospective tracking is sealed
E-DER is being tracked forward on data that no historical test has touched. Those results are not summarised, not totalled and not published — here or anywhere — until the evaluator opens them at a pre-declared sample size.

05 Current position

Not killed. Not blessed.

E-DER has survived the tests that have been run, which earns it a forward test and nothing else. It has not been promoted to paper, it does not size a position, and no part of Eclipse is authorised to trade it.

If the forward test settles the question, the answer will be recorded whichever way it goes.

ECLIPSE

An agent-driven quantitative trading intelligence system.

Alpha research, global market context, continuous backtesting, risk authorization and secure execution run as separate agents on one event bus. A Master Center keeps the registry, enforces the policy, and records why every decision was made — including the decisions that ended in no.

01 Master Center

The Master Center never places a trade.

It knows which agents are running, what each one is allowed to touch, and why every decision was made. Coordination is its only job.

Agents come and go. Strategies are frozen, re-opened and retired. The registry is the one place where that history stays legible, so a question asked six months from now still has an answer.

02 Global event bus

Agents publish. They do not call each other.

Direct calls between agents look convenient and then quietly become the architecture. Once Alpha imports Risk, you cannot replace either one without touching the other.

Eclipse routes everything through a subject-addressed bus. An agent declares what it publishes and what it subscribes to; the Master Center grants or denies that access. Adding an agent means adding a subscriber, not editing a caller.

03 The path of a trade

Five separations, one direction.

A signal cannot become an order by itself. Between the idea and the money there are four independent parties, each able to stop it, none able to skip the one in front.

Most paths end early. That is the design working, not the design failing.

04 Alpha Engine

Reads the tape. Proposes. Stops there.

The Alpha Engine is where Eclipse looks at market microstructure — forced liquidations, order book state, trade flow, event detection — and produces a single artifact: a trade candidate.

A candidate carries the arm that produced it, the anchor it fired from, and the context at that moment. It carries no order, no size and no route. Sizing belongs to Risk; routing belongs to Execution.

Microstructure

Liquidation pressure, book state, trade flow, and the shape of a cascade as it happens.

Event detection

Anchors are identified from observed flow, never from what the market did afterwards.

Arms, not settings

Each hypothesis is a named, versioned, frozen arm. Changing one creates a new arm.

No keys

The Alpha Engine has never held an exchange credential and is not permitted to.

05 Global Market Intelligence

Context, not signals.

Market Intelligence watches the world outside the order book: rates, currencies, indices, commodities, central banks, the economic calendar, volatility and the news cycle. It publishes one thing — state.

It is deliberately barred from changing an alpha rule. A regime read that has not been researched is an opinion, and opinions do not get to move thresholds.

Context can confirm a candidate or flag it for Risk. It cannot generate one, and it cannot loosen a rule. Anything stronger has to survive the research pipeline first — observe, store, research, validate, and only then consider production.

06 Research & backtest

A laboratory that is allowed to say no to itself.

The Research Engine runs continuously: validate the data, snapshot the dataset, backtest, walk forward, test out of sample, stress it, compare against what was claimed before, and write a report whether the answer is good or not.

Win rate is the least interesting output. An arm has to survive tail risk, cost sensitivity, regime change and sample size before anyone talks about promoting it.

08 Execution Gateway

The only component that talks to an exchange.

The gateway accepts approved orders and nothing else. It holds the trading credentials, and it is the single place in Eclipse where they exist.

Venues sit behind adapters, so adding one is an adapter and a set of tests — not a change to any strategy.

Venue adapters
BinanceBybitOKX + future venues
Credential isolation
Alpha, Research and Market Intelligence run in a network that cannot reach the exchange endpoints and cannot read the secret store. This is enforced by the network and the secret manager, not by convention.

09 Security Guardian

Deny by default.

Security is not a phase at the end. Every new agent is born with no permissions and earns each one explicitly: an identity, a secret policy, a network policy, the subjects it may read, the subjects it may write, and its logging obligations.

An agent that has not been granted a subject cannot see it on the bus at all.

10 Data Guardian

A backtest is only as honest as its clock.

The Data Guardian looks for the failures that quietly invalidate research: gaps in a websocket stream, out-of-order events, timestamp drift, revised history, duplicate fills, stale symbols — and above all, information that could not have been known yet.

Feeds do die. Eclipse marks those windows and refuses to draw conclusions across them rather than pretending the market was quiet.

11 PR Guardian

One reviewer, every repository.

PR Guardian runs at the organization level rather than being copied into each repo. When anyone opens a pull request it reads the diff, runs the tests, and reports what changed in terms of system behaviour — so two people working in different repositories still see each other's work.

Alongside the usual checks it looks for the mistakes specific to a trading system.

13 Dynamic agents

Some agents exist only while the event does.

Persistent agents are always running. Event-spawned agents are registered on demand, given a narrow scope and a lifetime, and retired when the event closes — with the same identity, permission and logging requirements as anything permanent.

A central bank meeting or a liquidation cascade is a temporary problem that deserves temporary specialists, not a permanent subsystem.

Research programme

Research

Eclipse keeps a written record of what it believes, how strongly, and what would change its mind. This page describes the concepts and the standard of evidence. It does not publish rules, thresholds or results.

What is public here
Concepts, status and sample-size class. Entry and exit rules, feature definitions, thresholds and measured performance stay on the private network.

01 Standard of evidence

Most of the work is finding out that something is not true.

A backtest that looks good on the sample it was chosen from is not evidence. Eclipse treats a historical result as a reason to run a test, never as a result.

The programme is organised around the ways a quantitative claim usually turns out to be wrong: lookahead, selection across many attempts, a sample that has already been searched, and a feed that was dead when the market looked quiet.

Before a finding counts
Causality
every input available at decision time — no exceptions, no "close enough"
Pre-registration
metric, sample size and stopping rule frozen and signed before the first look
Multiplicity
judged against the whole programme of attempts, not the one that worked
Out of sample
a period never used to choose anything
Cost
a conservative fee assumption, stated openly in every report
Data integrity
known outage windows excluded rather than read as calm markets
Sealed forward evaluation
Forward results are sealed while they accumulate. No running totals, no interim win rates, no comparisons against a control arm — not even on request. A sealed arm is opened by the evaluator at pre-declared sample sizes, and the only thing reported is whether the boundary was crossed. Peeking is what the seal exists to prevent.

02 Frozen arms

Three definitions, tracked side by side.

E-DER exists in three frozen variants. They are deliberately not versions of one another — loosening a condition creates a new arm, and the old arm keeps running. None supersedes, none replaces, and none is promoted because it currently looks better.

Frozen

E-DER V1

The strictest definition. Every condition present, including prior stress. The smallest population and the cleanest one.

25 events6 cascades

E-DER V2 · A2

One condition removed — prior stress no longer required. Tests whether the strict gate was doing work or only shrinking the sample.

 Research

E-DER V3

A2 plus a near-queue recovery condition. The broadest arm, and the one with the most room to be fooling itself.

198 events10 cascades

Event counts are population sizes, not results. A larger population is not a better arm — it is a different question.

03 Open hypotheses

Ideas, not strategies.

These are structural questions about how a market absorbs forced flow. None of them is a live strategy and several will end up refuted — which is a normal outcome and gets written down the same way a positive one would.

Coordinated capitulation deceleration

Forced selling arrives in bursts that slow before they stop. Does the deceleration carry information the level does not?

Hypothesis

Event–response–relaxation

Treat a cascade as an impulse and study the relaxation that follows it rather than the impulse itself.

Hypothesis

Multi-timescale sign reversal

Whether agreement or disagreement of direction across timescales separates exhaustion from continuation.

Hypothesis

Cumulative exhaustion hazard

Model the end of forced flow as a hazard rate that accumulates, instead of a threshold that trips.

Hypothesis

Cascade beta timing

How a symbol's sensitivity to a market-wide cascade changes over the life of that cascade.

Hypothesis

Liquidity resilience

How quickly a book rebuilds after being cleared, and whether the rebuild rate is stable enough to lean on.

Hypothesis

Fresh vs chronic stress

Whether a market entering stress behaves differently from one that has been under stress for hours.

Hypothesis

Price acceptance

Distinguishing a level that was traded through from a level the market actually accepted.

Hypothesis

Flow relaxation

The shape of order flow decay after a shock, independent of where the price ended up.

Hypothesis

Cross-asset confirmation

Whether the same structure appearing in an unrelated symbol raises or lowers confidence.

04 A burned sample

You can run out of questions before you run out of ideas.

A fixed history supports a limited number of independent tests. Every additional feature, threshold and variant spends part of that budget, and the budget does not grow because the ideas are good.

Once a sample has been searched heavily, "let's try the next indicator" stops being research. Eclipse measures how much of the sample has been consumed and treats the answer as binding.

Work that does not spend the sample
Replication
the same frozen rule, on a symbol not used to discover it
Audit
accounting and data-integrity checks on results already claimed
Correction
re-judging past findings against the full count of attempts
Duration
anything that adds new time — the denominator grows, the numerator does not
Cross-section, done once
Testing many symbols and keeping the best one is the same mistake with more steps. A cross-sectional test is pre-registered as a single pooled test with a stated sign convention, and it reports whatever it finds.

05 The graveyard

Refuted ideas are kept, not deleted.

Every idea that failed stays on the record with the condition that killed it. The register exists so that the same idea does not get rediscovered, re-tested and re-believed a few months later under a new name.

Re-opening an entry requires a reason that did not exist when it was closed — new data, a corrected measurement, or a demonstrated flaw in the original test. Enthusiasm is not a reason.

Closed families
Buy-side fade Reversal selection Cross-asset transfer Micro-timing Tight stops Partial exits Limit entry Quote-quality harvesting

Each entry carries the test that closed it and the condition under which it could be re-opened.
